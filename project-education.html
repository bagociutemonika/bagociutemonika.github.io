Project 02 · Education Research · 2025 – present

Evaluating bilingual education programmes

As Lead Researcher at Nuffic, I designed and now run a large-scale evaluation of bilingual education outcomes across more than 20 schools, covering language skills, personal development, and global citizenship.

Questionnaire designPower simulation Stratified samplingMultilevel modellingPower BI

ContextThe question

Is bilingual education delivering on its three core promises?

Nuffic needed credible, programme-level evidence on whether bilingual secondary education in the Netherlands actually improves language skills, personal development, and global citizenship, not just whether schools believed it did. That meant building a measurement approach from scratch, one that could be trusted across more than 20 different schools with different student populations.

MethodHow I approached it

Building, testing, and validating a custom instrument

I constructed and piloted a questionnaire (n = 100) to establish internal consistency and reliability before full deployment, drawing on established frameworks, including the IB Learner Profile, to operationalise personal development. I ran power simulation studies to determine the sample size needed, then recruited more than 20 schools using a stratified sampling strategy to ensure the final sample fairly represented different school types.

  • Piloted the instrument before full deployment to catch reliability issues early
  • Used power simulations, not guesswork, to size the study properly
  • Stratified sampling to avoid over-representing any one type of school

AnalysisMaking sense of it

Multilevel modelling across schools and groups

Because students are nested within schools, a standard regression approach would understate uncertainty and risk misleading conclusions. I applied multilevel modelling, alongside t-tests and ANOVA where appropriate, to properly account for school-level clustering and identify genuine differences in outcomes.

OutcomeWhat it produced

Findings stakeholders could actually use

I built dashboards and visual reports (Power BI, Canva) to translate the statistical findings into recommendations for teachers, school coordinators, and policymakers, communicating not just results but the methodological assumptions and limitations behind them, so stakeholders knew exactly how much weight the findings could bear.

ReflectionWhat I'd do differently

Honest limitations

Self-reported measures of confidence and personal development are useful but imperfect proxies, they capture perception, not always behaviour. A stronger design would pair the survey with some direct behavioural or performance measures to triangulate the self-report data, something I would build into a future iteration of this study.

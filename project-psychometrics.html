Project 03 · Psychometrics & Machine Learning · 2025 – 2026

Detecting item drift in large-scale assessments

As Data Analyst Intern at CITO, I built a simulation study modelled on PISA-style assessments to test whether machine learning can reliably detect bias in test items, benchmarked against traditional statistical methods.

IRTDIFSimulation Machine learningLogistic regression

ContextThe question

Can machine learning detect unfair test items as reliably as traditional methods?

Large-scale assessments need every test item to measure the same thing, fairly, for every test-taker. When an item behaves differently for otherwise similar test-takers, this is called differential item functioning (DIF), and it threatens the validity of the whole assessment. Traditional detection methods work, but are often slow at scale. The question was whether machine learning could match or improve on their accuracy.

MethodHow I approached it

Simulating assessments with known, controlled flaws

I designed a simulation study imitating the structure of large-scale international assessments, generating data under Rasch and 2PL item response models. I then deliberately introduced controlled violations of unidimensionality and injected differential item functioning into specific items, creating a dataset where I knew exactly which items were flawed and how, the ground truth needed to properly evaluate any detection method.

  • Simulated data under two different IRT models for robustness
  • Injected known DIF and unidimensionality violations as ground truth
  • Varied conditions systematically to stress-test detection methods

AnalysisBenchmarking methods

Machine learning versus logistic regression

I applied machine learning models and compared their performance directly against logistic regression, the traditional approach, to determine which method detected the known, injected DIF most reliably across varying conditions. This comparative framing mattered: the goal was not to prove machine learning works, but to honestly evaluate whether it outperforms the established method.

OutcomeWhat it produced

A scientific paper on method reliability

I communicated the findings and methodological trade-offs in a scientific paper, documenting not just which method performed better, but under which conditions, and why, information that matters for anyone deciding which method to trust in practice.

ReflectionWhat I'd do differently

Honest limitations

Simulated data, however carefully constructed, is not real-world data. The next step would be validating these findings against a real assessment dataset, where noise, missingness, and unmodelled complexity could behave differently than in a clean simulation.

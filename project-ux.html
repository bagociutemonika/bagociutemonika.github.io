Designing for discovery: a platform for amateur artists

An independent product project connecting amateur artists with venues, such as cafes, willing to exhibit their work. I own the concept and development end to end, and use structured usability research to make sure the platform is actually understood by the people it is built for.

Usability testingModerated & unmoderated research Affinity diagrammingFigmaProduct design

ContextThe question

Could people understand what the platform was for, just by using it?

The core idea was simple: give amateur artists, people without gallery representation or an existing following, an easy way to find real-world spaces, like cafes and small venues, willing to exhibit their work. But a simple idea does not guarantee a clear interface. I needed to know whether first-time visitors could understand the platform's purpose and navigate it without guidance, before investing further in development.

MethodHow I approached it

Moderated and unmoderated usability research

I ran moderated sessions with early users, observing them attempt core tasks (understanding what the platform does, finding a venue, starting an application) while thinking aloud, so I could see exactly where confusion happened in real time. I paired this with unmoderated testing, letting other users complete the same tasks independently, to check whether the issues I observed held up without my presence influencing behaviour.

  • Defined core tasks and a simple think-aloud protocol for moderated sessions
  • Ran unmoderated sessions to validate findings without facilitator influence
  • Recorded observations systematically across both formats

SynthesisMaking sense of it

Affinity diagramming to find the real patterns

Rather than treating each observation in isolation, I clustered notes from both moderated and unmoderated sessions using affinity diagramming, grouping recurring friction points and confusions into themes. This made it possible to separate one-off confusion from genuine, repeated usability issues worth acting on.

OutcomeWhat changed

From insight to iteration

The clustered findings directly shaped the next design iteration, which I prototyped in Figma. I then followed up with further monitoring to see whether the changes actually resolved the confusion observed in the first round, closing the loop between research and design rather than treating testing as a one-off checkbox.

ReflectionWhat I'd do differently

Honest limitations

This was a small, early-stage sample, not yet representative of the platform's eventual two-sided audience (artists and venue owners), and I have so far tested primarily one side of that relationship in depth. The next round needs to bring venue owners into the same rigorous process, since their understanding of the platform matters just as much as the artists'.

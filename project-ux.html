Project 01 · UX Research · 2025 – present

Designing for discovery: a platform for amateur artists

An independent product project connecting amateur artists with venues, such as cafes, willing to exhibit their work. I own the concept and development end to end, and use usability research to make sure the platform is actually understood by the people it is built for.

PlanSetting up the research

Could people understand what the platform was for, just by using it?

The idea started with a simple observation: some cafes display local art, others don't, even though many cafe managers said they'd be open to it if approached. There was no real obstacle on their side, just no easy way for artists and venues to find each other. The platform is designed to close that gap, giving artists a way to get seen and sold, venues a low-effort way to host art that can also attract customers, and eventually, customers a way to discover and buy work directly. This first phase focuses on validating the core communication loop between artists and venues.

Research goals

Validate whether both sides of the platform, artists and venue managers, understand its purpose and can complete their core tasks without guidance.

Research questions

  • Do artists understand how to create a profile and apply to a venue without guidance?
  • Do cafe managers understand how to browse, select, and approve an artist without guidance?
  • Where do each of these groups hesitate, get confused, or abandon the task?
  • Does the platform's value proposition come through clearly on first impression?

KPIs tracked

Time on task User error rate Drop-off rate SUS

Methodology

Unmoderated usability testing. Participants were given the platform with minimal framing and asked to think aloud as they explored.

Participants

Four participants across two groups: two cafe managers and two art school students.

ReflectionWhat I'd do differently

Honest limitations

The unscripted approach surfaced genuine, organic confusion, but made it harder to compare results consistently across participants. The sample size is also too small to support any statistical inference. This also revealed a gap in the methodology itself: I only gave participants a few prompts, to keep the session open-ended, but in hindsight, more prompts would have let them navigate further and engage with more of the platform.
